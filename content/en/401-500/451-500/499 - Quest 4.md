Quest (4)

Melbes, the Noble Alliance of Various Races.

After joining them, I had secured support for both House Jandel and Clan Anabada.

In the clan’s case, I had practically begged for their support, and what they provided wasn’t particularly substantial.

The main benefits were a reduced price on the clan house we currently used as our residence and access to future loans.

‘Requests...’

That was why these ‘requests’ had become so important.

‘Requests’ were closer to conditional contracts than outright benefits.

‘How well I handle this request will determine my standing and the benefits I receive. If I perform well, my position will rise significantly.’

In gaming terms, it was similar to a reputation system.

Completing quests increased your rank, and with it, the benefits you received. Of course, I still had to know my limits.

“What kind of request?”

Rather than asking about the reward, I first wanted to know the details of the request.

“Information.”

“...Information?”

This was completely unexpected.

Requests usually fell into one of three categories: hunting, gathering, or combat.

But information?

I gave him a look, urging him to explain further. Lord Goodrix sipped his tea and continued.

“Well... there’s been quite a big incident recently, hasn’t there?”

“Stop beating around the bush and get to the point.”

The Viscount, who had initially tried to speak in a roundabout, noble manner, coughed awkwardly and got straight to the point.

Well, not exactly straight to the point.

“The inscription on the Stone of Honor. After some investigation, it seems that the hidden area likely refers to a new layer.”

Now I could understand what he was getting at.

“Baron Jandel, you’re planning to search unexplored areas in the next expedition, right? We’d like you to share your findings with us. Whether the hidden area is indeed a new layer, where the entrance is, how to get in, and what’s inside.”

Lord Goodrix paused for a moment, as if organizing his thoughts, then continued.

“Baron, do you know what the most expensive Level 9 Essence in history is? The Abyssal Fish. Although it isn’t significantly better than other Level 9 Essences, it sold for 3.2 billion stones. Do you know why?”

It was the first time I’d heard this story, but I could guess the reason.

“Because it was the first.”

“Exactly. The fact that it was the first discovery from a 9th-floor Rift was enough for the Mage Tower to pay such a staggering sum. Being first carries immense value.”

When I asked if it was all about money, the lord shook his head.

“Throughout history, newly discovered areas have always held countless treasures and opportunities. Hero Mulmarin, the Dragon Slayer, was a prime example. The power and privileges he gained from being the first to slay a dragon were things no one in the Labyrinth had managed to replicate since.”

“So?”

“The information we receive from you will be passed on to trusted explorers from the various races.”

“You want to use the information to help them grow stronger.”

“Exactly. The more power the explorers from various races gain, the greater our power will become.”

Although the Viscount’s explanation was unnecessarily long, I could clearly see why Melbes had made the request.

And I was convinced.

“Of course, Baron, you may find it unfair to share valuable expedition findings with us. But I promise you this: the information you provide will only be shared with trusted people who will keep it confidential.”

With that, the Viscount asked if I would accept the request, and I replied.

“Alright. I’ll take the request.”

After all, it wasn’t a quest with any penalty for failure.

I had nothing to lose by accepting it.

“Then I’ll see you later.”

After leaving the Viscount’s estate and returning home, I gathered my team to share the details of the new quest.

There were a few concerns, but no objections.

We discussed our future expedition plans late into the night before finally heading to bed.

Amelia and Erwin sat in two chairs in front of my bed.

“There’s no need for you to stay, Tercia.”

“No way! I’m here to watch over you, mister!”

Why were they acting like this when I would only be gone for a short while?

Tick, tock.

I closed my eyes, counting the seconds on the clock on my bedside table.
